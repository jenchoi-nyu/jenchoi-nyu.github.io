Week 1 — Proposal: Shaping Story Through Sound

Elevator Pitch

Echo + Form explores how technology can transform pottery into a deeply personal, interactive experience. By integrating personal data—such as memories, emotions, and gestures—into the creation of ceramic vessels, this project turns objects into storytelling artifacts that hold emotional weight over time. It merges craft, technology, and emotional reflection to create meaningful, interactive objects that resonate on both a personal and collective level.

Key Elements

  • How emotions and memories shape objects’ meaning
  • The role of interactive technology in craft and storytelling
  • Pottery as a medium for personal and cultural identity

Audience

  • People interested in personal storytelling and emotional expression
  • Artists, designers, and technologists exploring new forms of interactive craft
  • Educators and researchers in design, material culture, and digital fabrication

Research Conducted

  • Emotional Objects & Museums: Observations from the 9/11 Memorial Museum, exploring how artifacts carry both personal and collective significance
  • Affect & Cultural Theory: Engaged with Sara Ahmed’s affect theory and Stuart Hall’s encoding/decoding framework to understand how meaning is assigned to material objects
  • Pottery & Personalization: Studies on how intention, personalization, and tactile engagement in ceramics enhance emotional connection

What I’m Making (WIP)

  • Interactive Clay Vessels: Using personal inputs (voice, heartbeat, gestures) to shape digital vessel forms
  • 3D-Printed Slip-Casting Molds: Enabling broader accessibility in personalized ceramic creation
  • Digital Models: Designing a real-time interface for users to generate their own vessels through sound-driven algorithms

Why I’m Making This

The motivation behind Echo + Form lies in exploring how everyday objects can become emotional artifacts—vessels not just in form, but in function, capable of holding memory, identity, and presence. This project bridges traditional craft and emerging technologies, revealing how deeply personal data can be embedded in a physical material like clay. Ultimately, it asks: what does it mean to give shape to feeling, and how might that form live with us?

Week 2 — Structuring the Journey: The Plan Behind the Clay

Objective

This week focused on designing a clear plan for how Echo + Form will unfold over the semester. The project aims to explore how emotions, personal data, and interactive technology can shape ceramic vessels, transforming them into meaningful storytelling artifacts. With this goal in mind, I created a timeline, mapped deliverables, and began defining the relationship between voice, technology, and form.

Deliverables & Key Milestones

  • March 13: Midterm Demo Day
  • May 8: Final Critiques
  • May 9: Public Showcase

Schedule

Phase 1: Concept Development & Refinement (Weeks 1–4)

  • Week 1–2: Research & Ideation — grounding the work in theory and emotional storytelling through objects
  • Week 3–4: Interaction Concepts — exploring data input methods such as voice, motion, heartbeat, and narrative memory

Phase 2: Prototype Development & Testing (Weeks 5–12)

  • Week 5–6: Refine concepts and define user interaction flow
  • Week 7–8: Build early prototypes using voice-to-form translation and 3D-printable models
  • Week 9: Internal review and refinement of interaction quality
  • Week 10–11: Conduct structured user testing with participants to gather emotional and usability insights
  • Week 12: Finalize vessel design logic and polish technical execution

Phase 3: Final Presentation & Showcase (Weeks 13–15)

  • Week 13: Prepare polished presentation and documentation
  • Week 14: Apply final refinements, prepare for public installation
  • Week 15: Present final work and showcase interactive experience

Reflections

Having a structure has brought clarity to both my process and my purpose. Echo + Form is not just about creating beautiful vessels, but about crafting a design journey that enables others to see, feel, and shape their emotional imprint in clay. This planning phase helped me think beyond individual components—voice, clay, tech—and focus on the holistic experience. From interaction flow to exhibition format, each step is designed to guide the user in crafting a vessel that feels like a piece of themselves.

Week 3 — On Feedback, Vulnerability, and Finding Form

Resources

  • Conversation: Critique by Eva Sutton, from The Art of Critical Making (2013)
  • “Find the Coaching in Criticism” by Sheila Heen and Douglas Stone (Harvard Business Review, 2014)
  • Thanks for the Feedback | Doug Stone & Sheila Heen | Talks at Google

Reflections

This week, I confronted the dual nature of feedback—its ability to both clarify and destabilize. I shared my concept with peers and mentors: a system where voice, music, and personal story shape a ceramic vessel. The idea felt abstract to some and deeply resonant to others. The mixed reactions taught me that presenting a hybrid craft-tech-emotion project requires a clear explanation of both process and purpose.

I asked for feedback on several fronts: Does it make sense that a voice could shape form? Are the input methods (motion, heartbeat, questionnaire) too varied or just right? Does this actually communicate emotion—or just represent it? I wanted to know whether people felt connected to the idea, or distanced by the tech. The responses helped illuminate where the project succeeds (personal resonance, materiality, storytelling) and where it needs clarity (interaction flow, material feasibility, technical precision).

The Type of Information I’m Looking For

  • Concept Clarity: Does the narrative of emotion-to-object transformation come through?
  • User Interaction: Are input methods intuitive and compelling?
  • Material & Technical Feasibility: Does the hybrid of digital design and clay 3D printing align with craft tradition?
  • Emotional Impact: Does this feel like more than just a digital fabrication experiment?

My Past Relationship with Feedback

I’ve always valued specific, actionable feedback. I thrive when critiques point out what works and why, what’s unclear and how to rethink it. I struggle when responses are too vague (“It’s cool!”) or contradictory. To navigate this, I’ve learned to ask better questions—direct, specific, and intentional. This week was a reminder that design is not just making, it’s conversing: with your audience, your materials, and your own intuition.

Takeaway

Vulnerability is part of creative growth. Feedback invited me to listen more closely—not only to others, but to what the project is becoming. This week, Echo + Form began to solidify not just in structure, but in spirit. I’m learning to trust the shape that’s emerging through iteration, conversation, and reflection.

Week 4 — Building the Theoretical Foundation

Resources

  • Past Thesis Examples
  • Thesis Examples Drive

Table of Contents

  1. Introduction
    Overview of Shape of Your Voice, a project exploring the intersection of technology, emotions, interactivity, and pottery. The section introduces the significance of clay as a historical storytelling medium and sets up the thesis statement: to bridge intangible emotion and physical form.
  2. Literature Review & Theoretical Foundations
    A review of affect theory and material culture. Draws from Sara Ahmed’s writing on emotion as both individual and shared, and Stuart Hall’s encoding/decoding to explain how meaning is embedded in cultural artifacts. Artworks by Roman Ondák and Jennifer Rochlin ground the conversation in the power of bodily imprint and collective identity.
  3. Design Methodology & Concept Development
    Explains how vocal sound waves (pitch, cadence, volume) will be translated into parametric forms using code. Describes how personalization is central, with users contributing their names and stories as inputs into the design of their vessels.
  4. Prototyping & Interaction Design
    Details the interaction system for voice input and real-time model generation. Introduces tools such as GCODE, parametric modeling platforms, and 3D printing methods. This section also discusses the design of the digital interface for users to participate in shaping their vessels.
  5. Testing & Iteration
    Lays out a plan for gathering user feedback on emotional resonance, usability, and personalization. This part explains how prototypes will be refined through cycles of testing and interpretation.
  6. Final Design & Outcomes
    Describes what the final exhibition will include: physical vessels shaped by voice and song, a digital kiosk to contribute new stories, and documentation of the user journey. Focuses on the emotional impact of the final artifacts and their resonance as personal and collective portraits.
  7. Appendices
    Supporting material such as prototype images, sound recordings, user testing results, and scholarly references.

Reflections

This week I committed to the academic backbone of Echo + Form. It was both empowering and intimidating to define the theoretical lineage my work is in conversation with. As I outlined the paper structure, I realized how critical it is to situate personal design work within broader cultural frameworks—how emotion, voice, clay, and memory intersect through craft and theory.

Sara Ahmed’s discussion of affect helped validate that the personal is inherently collective. Jennifer Rochlin’s work reinforced the body as a tool of inscription. I found myself thinking more about how a name isn’t just a sound—it’s a lived story. The vessel becomes a container for that sound, that story, that self.

This outline isn’t just for academic rigor—it’s a compass. It ensures the work remains rooted in meaning, even as the form continues to evolve.
